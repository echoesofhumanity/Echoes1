🔎 Investigative Journalism

Research-based journalism examining complex humanitarian, social and public-interest issues.

Investigative journalism seeks to understand what lies beneath the surface of important stories through evidence, documentation, careful research and responsible reporting.

Why Investigative Journalism Matters

Some of the most important humanitarian stories cannot be understood through a single announcement or event. They require time, research, documentation and persistence.

Investigative journalism can help reveal patterns, examine systems, document failures and bring important public-interest questions into the open.

🔎 Evidence

Important findings should be supported by documents, reliable sources, records and other available evidence.

🧠 Analysis

Complex subjects require careful analysis rather than assumptions or conclusions based on incomplete information.

🌍 Public Interest

Investigations should serve a legitimate public-interest purpose and contribute to greater understanding.

❤️ Human Impact

Behind every investigation are real people whose dignity, safety and privacy must remain important.

🔬 Investigative Process

Responsible investigations require a structured process designed to reduce errors and protect the people involved.

1. Research Question

Each investigation begins by defining the subject, question and public-interest purpose.

2. Source Discovery

Researchers identify relevant documents, records, interviews, testimony and other sources.

3. Verification

Important information is checked against independent and reliable evidence wherever possible.

4. Documentation

Evidence and sources are organized so that important findings can be understood and reviewed.

5. Analysis

Researchers examine relationships, patterns, inconsistencies and possible explanations.

6. Editorial Review

Findings are reviewed for accuracy, context, fairness, safety and responsible communication.

Investigation Areas

Investigative work may examine a wide range of humanitarian and public-interest subjects.

🧒 Protection of Children

Research concerning systems, failures and circumstances affecting the protection and wellbeing of children.

⚖️ Human Rights

Examination of issues affecting fundamental rights, dignity and human wellbeing.

🏛️ Institutions

Public-interest research examining institutional decisions, systems and accountability.

🌍 Humanitarian Crises

Investigations into complex circumstances surrounding humanitarian emergencies and their consequences.

💻 Digital Society

Research into digital communication, online systems, misinformation and their effects on people.

🗂️ Historical Records

Examination of historical documents and records that may help explain important humanitarian questions.

📚 Sources & Documentation

Investigative journalism depends on the careful evaluation of different types of information.

📄 Public Records

Official documents, reports, public databases and other accessible records may provide important evidence.

🎙️ Interviews

Interviews can provide firsthand perspectives and help researchers understand events and experiences.

📷 Visual Evidence

Photographs, video and other visual records may contribute to the documentation of events.

🔬 Research

Academic research and specialist knowledge can provide context and help test important claims.

⚖️ Investigative Ethics

The pursuit of important information must never become an excuse for unnecessary harm.

❤️ Human Dignity

People involved in an investigation must be treated as human beings rather than simply as sources or subjects.

🔐 Privacy

Personal information should be protected whenever publication is not necessary for legitimate public interest.

🛡️ Safety

Investigators must consider possible risks to sources, communities, contributors and other people involved.

⚖️ Fairness

Serious allegations require careful evidence, appropriate context and responsible presentation.

🔄 Corrections & Transparency

Investigative journalism must remain open to correction. New evidence, errors or incomplete information may require a published clarification or correction.

Truth Requires Accountability

Responsible journalism does not claim perfection. It accepts correction when evidence shows that a published statement was incomplete or incorrect.
